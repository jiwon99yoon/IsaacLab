Title: Motion Planning-Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning with
      Contact-Aware Reward Shaping for High-DOF Dexterous Manipulation

Author: [Name]
Date: January 2025

================================================================================
1. MOTIVATION
================================================================================

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated human-level performance in
dexterous manipulation tasks [1,2], but suffers from extreme sample
inefficiency. For example, OpenAI Dactyl [2] required 100 years worth of
simulation to learn cube rotation. This severely hinders deployment of RL on
real robotic systems.


1.1 Prior Solutions and Research Gap
-------------------------

Recent works have proposed bootstrapping RL with automatic demonstrations
generated from motion planning [3,4,5]. Abbatematteo et al. [3] showed that
kinematic planning-based demonstrations outperform random initialization, and
PLANRL [4] achieved 90% success rate on real robots. However, these approaches
were limited to robotic arms with simple grippers (6-7 DOF only).

Scaling to high-DOF dexterous hands (20+ DOF) presents three challenges: (1)
3-4x larger action space making exploration difficult, (2) kinematic planners
cannot capture contact dynamics, (3) multi-finger coordination is required but
kinematics alone cannot ensure stable grasping. Therefore, this research aims
to extend motion planning-bootstrapped RL to high-DOF hands and improve sample
efficiency through contact-aware reward shaping.


================================================================================
2. PROBLEM DESCRIPTION
================================================================================

This research uses a UR10e robotic arm (6-DOF) combined with two dexterous
hands: UR10e-DG5F (26-DOF, fully-actuated 5-finger gripper) and UR10e-Inspire
(12-DOF, under-actuated 5-finger gripper). We learn various object lifting
tasks and train RL-Games PPO with GPU acceleration (4096 parallel environments)
in the Isaac Lab environment.


2.1 Three-Phase Framework
------------------

**Phase 1: Automated Demonstration Generation (Isaac Sim + MoveIt2)**
We use the MoveIt2 motion planner to plan collision-free trajectories to target
poses, execute them in Isaac Sim, and record (state, action) pairs. We generate
1000 demonstrations per robot and convert them to Isaac Lab format.

**Phase 2: Behavior Cloning Pre-training**
We pre-train a policy on demonstration data using a 3-layer FC network (256-256-
128 units). We train for 50 epochs with MSE loss, targeting >30% success rate.

**Phase 3: RL Fine-tuning + Contact-Aware Rewards**
After initializing with the BC policy, we perform PPO training with contact-
specific rewards (5000 epochs). We maintain a demonstration buffer and train
with 25% demo + 75% RL data.


2.2 Core Contribution: Contact-Aware Reward Shaping
---------------------------------

To address shortcut behaviors in dexterous manipulation RL (arm pushing,
exploiting floor bouncing, etc.), we introduce three contact-specific rewards:

(1) **Contact Gating:** Position tracking reward activated only during
    finger-object contact
    R_gated = R_position × I(F_contact > 0.1N)

(2) **Grasp Duration:** Encourage sustained contact across multiple fingers
    (minimum 10 steps)
    R_duration = (1/N_fingers) × Σⱼ I(sustained_contact_j)

(3) **Ground Penalty:** Penalize object-floor collisions
    R_ground = -0.5 × I(F_object_z > 1.0N)

Total reward: R_total = 2.0·R_gated + 1.0·R_duration + R_ground + R_baseline

**Key Insight:** Motion planning provides kinematic feasibility while contact
rewards provide dynamic stability, complementing each other to enable robust
grasping.


2.3 Experimental Design
------------

We conduct an ablation study with 4 variants: (1) Pure PPO (baseline), (2) BC +
PPO, (3) PPO + Contact, (4) BC + PPO + Contact (full method). We run each
variant on 2 robots for a total of 8 experiments, evaluating success rate,
sample efficiency (samples needed to reach 80% success), contact force
statistics, and grasp quality. We expect the full method to achieve 2-4x faster
convergence and 80%+ success rate compared to pure PPO.


================================================================================
3. EXPECTED CONTRIBUTION
================================================================================

3.1 Theoretical/Algorithmic Contributions
-------------------------

**(1) Scaling to High-DOF Systems:** We extend motion planning-bootstrapped RL
[3] from 7-DOF robotic arms to 26-DOF dexterous hands, validating that sample
efficiency gains are maintained in high-dimensional spaces.

**(2) Kinematic-Dynamic Complementarity:** We demonstrate that combining
kinematic planning (collision avoidance, feasibility) with contact rewards
(stable grasping, finger coordination) addresses the limitations of each
approach individually.

**(3) Automated Demonstration Pipeline:** We present a fully automated
demonstration generation method without human teleoperation ($10,000+ hardware).

**(4) Contact-Aware Reward Framework:** We provide systematic reward shaping
(contact gating, duration, penalty) for dexterous manipulation.


3.2 Practical Contributions and Impact
----------------------

**(1) Sample Efficiency Improvement:** While pure PPO requires ~100M samples
and 24 hours to reach 80% success, our full method achieves this with 25-50M
samples in 6-12 hours, providing 2-4x faster convergence and 50-75% training
time reduction.

**(2) Cost Reduction:** Compared to human demonstration collection ($10,000+
hardware, 8-83 hours of human time) or pure RL (100 years simulation, $3,500+
electricity), automated demonstration generation (hardware $0, 1 hour setup,
4-8 hours automated generation) provides substantial savings.

**(3) Real-World Deployment Validation:** We validate sim-to-real transfer by
deploying policies learned on the UR10e-based system to real robots,
demonstrating rapid adaptation to novel objects through automatic demonstration
generation and fast training in just a few hours.

**(4) Open-Source Contribution:** We provide a demonstration toolkit, contact-
aware reward library, and implementation guide for the Isaac Lab community to
accelerate reproducibility and real-world applications.


In the long term, this research will have scientific impact through: presenting
methodologies for improving sample efficiency in high-DOF robot learning,
enhancing theoretical understanding of motion planning and RL integration, and
providing reward shaping guidelines for contact manipulation tasks. It will
also have technological/economic impact through: improving practicality of
dexterous manipulation RL, expanding RL applications in robotics, and improving
accessibility of RL technology for SMEs.

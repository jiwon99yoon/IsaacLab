\section{Motivation}

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated human-level performance in dexterous manipulation \cite{rajeswaran2018learning, andrychowicz2020learning}, but suffers from extreme sample inefficiency. For example, OpenAI Dactyl \cite{andrychowicz2020learning} required 100 years worth of simulation to learn cube rotation, severely hindering real-world deployment.

Recent works have proposed bootstrapping RL with automatic demonstrations from motion planning \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping, bhaskar2024planrl, lian2023reinforcement}. Abbatematteo et al.\ \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping} showed that kinematic planning-based demonstrations outperform random initialization, and PLANRL \cite{bhaskar2024planrl} achieved 90\% success on real robots. However, these approaches were limited to robotic arms with simple grippers (6--7 DOF).

Scaling to high-DOF dexterous hands (20+ DOF) presents three challenges: (1) 3--4$\times$ larger action space making exploration difficult, (2) kinematic planners cannot capture contact dynamics, (3) multi-finger coordination is required but kinematics alone cannot ensure stable grasping. Therefore, this research aims to extend motion planning-bootstrapped RL to high-DOF hands and improve sample efficiency through contact-aware reward shaping.

\section{Problem Description}

This research uses a UR10e robotic arm (6-DOF) combined with two dexterous hands: \textbf{UR10e-DG5F} (26-DOF, fully-actuated 5-finger gripper) and \textbf{UR10e-Inspire} (12-DOF, under-actuated 5-finger gripper). Various object lifting tasks are learned and RL-Games PPO is trained with GPU acceleration (4096 parallel environments) in the Isaac Lab environment.

\subsection{Three-Phase Framework}

\textbf{Phase 1: Automated Demonstration Generation (Isaac Sim + MoveIt2).} The MoveIt2 motion planner is used to plan collision-free trajectories to target poses, which are then executed in Isaac Sim to record (state, action) pairs. 1000 demonstrations per robot are generated and converted to Isaac Lab format.

\par \noindent

\textbf{Phase 2: Behavior Cloning Pre-training.} A policy is pre-trained on demonstration data using a 3-layer FC network (256-256-128 units). Training proceeds for 50 epochs with MSE loss, targeting $>$30\% success rate.

\par \noindent

\textbf{Phase 3: RL Fine-tuning + Contact-Aware Rewards.} After initializing with the BC policy, PPO training is performed with contact-specific rewards (5000 epochs). A demonstration buffer is maintained and training proceeds with 25\% demo + 75\% RL data.

\subsection{Core Contribution: Contact-Aware Reward Shaping}

To address shortcut behaviors (arm pushing, floor bouncing), three contact-specific rewards are introduced: (1) \textbf{Contact Gating} activates position tracking only during finger-object contact, (2) \textbf{Grasp Duration} encourages sustained multi-finger contact (minimum 10 steps), (3) \textbf{Ground Penalty} penalizes object-floor collisions. \textbf{Key Insight:} Motion planning provides kinematic feasibility while contact rewards provide dynamic stability, enabling robust grasping.

\subsection{Experimental Design}

An ablation study is conducted with 4 variants: (1) Pure PPO (baseline), (2) BC + PPO, (3) PPO + Contact, (4) BC + PPO + Contact (full method). Each variant is run on 2 robots for a total of 8 experiments, evaluating success rate, sample efficiency (samples needed to reach 80\% success), contact forces, and grasp quality. The full method is expected to achieve 2--4$\times$ faster convergence and 80\%+ success rate compared to pure PPO.

\section{Expected Contribution}

\subsection{Theoretical/Algorithmic Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Scaling to High-DOF Systems:} Extends motion planning-bootstrapped RL \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping} from 7-DOF arms to 26-DOF dexterous hands, validating sample efficiency gains in high dimensions.

    \item \textbf{Kinematic-Dynamic Complementarity:} Demonstrates that combining kinematic planning (collision avoidance) with contact rewards (stable grasping) addresses individual limitations.

    \item \textbf{Automated Demonstration Pipeline:} Presents fully automated demonstration generation without human teleoperation.

    \item \textbf{Contact-Aware Reward Framework:} Provides systematic reward shaping for dexterous manipulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Practical Contributions and Impact}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Sample Efficiency:} Pure PPO requires $\sim$100M samples (24 hours) for 80\% success; the proposed method could achieve this with 25--50M samples (6--12 hours), providing 2--4$\times$ speedup and 50--75\% time reduction.

    \item \textbf{Cost Reduction:} Automated generation (\$0 hardware, under 10 hours total) versus human demonstrations (\$10,000+ hardware, under 100 hours) or pure RL (100 years simulation, \$5,000+ electricity).

    \item \textbf{Open-Source Toolkit:} Provides demonstration toolkit, reward library, and implementation guide for Isaac Lab community.
\end{enumerate}

This research advances sample efficiency in high-DOF robot learning, enhances understanding of motion planning and RL integration, provides reward shaping guidelines for contact manipulation, and improves accessibility of RL technology for practical robotics applications.

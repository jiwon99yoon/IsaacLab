1. MOTIVATION
================================================================================

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved remarkable success in dexterous
manipulation tasks, demonstrating human-level performance on complex in-hand
object reorientation [1,2]. However, these approaches suffer from extreme
sample inefficiency, often requiring hundreds of millions of environment
interactions before reaching reasonable performance. This limitation severely
hinders the deployment of RL in real-world robotic systems where data
collection is expensive and time-consuming.

Recent work has demonstrated that motion planning can be used to bootstrap RL
by generating kinematically feasible demonstrations automatically [3,4,5].
Abbatematteo et al. [3] showed that motion planning-based initialization
significantly outperforms random initialization and is competitive with human
demonstrations for robotic arm manipulation. PLANRL [4] extended this approach
with dynamic mode switching between classical planning and RL. However, these
prior works have exclusively focused on robotic arms (6-7 DOF) with simple
parallel-jaw grippers, leaving a critical gap in applying this methodology to
high-DOF dexterous hands (20+ DOF) for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The challenge of extending motion planning-bootstrapped RL to dexterous hands
stems from three key factors:

1) High-dimensional action space (20+ DOF vs 7 DOF in prior work)
2) Contact-rich dynamics that kinematic planners cannot capture
3) Multi-finger coordination requirements absent in simple grippers

We propose to bridge this gap by extending motion planning-bootstrapped RL to
high-DOF dexterous hands (26-DOF and 12-DOF) with novel contact-aware reward
shaping mechanisms. Our approach generates demonstrations via MoveIt2 motion
planner in Isaac Sim, then transfers them to Isaac Lab for GPU-accelerated RL
training with contact-specific rewards.

================================================================================
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
================================================================================

2.1 Motion Planning-Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning
-------------------------------------------------------
Abbatematteo et al. [3] first demonstrated that kinematic motion planning can
generate demonstrations to bootstrap RL without human input. They evaluated
three manipulation tasks (drawer opening, microwave closing, t-ball) with a
7-DOF KUKA arm in PyBullet, showing that motion planner initialization was
competitive with human demonstrations and significantly outperformed random
initialization.

PLANRL [4] recently extended this approach with a framework that dynamically
switches between classical motion planning (for reaching waypoints) and RL
(for fine-grained manipulation). They achieved 90% success rate on simple
real-world tasks with a UR3e arm and Robotiq parallel-jaw gripper.

SILP+ [5] proposed self-imitation learning where a motion planner generates
demonstrations from successful RL states, achieving 90% success on UR5e arm
motion planning tasks.

**Gap in Prior Work:** All prior motion planning-bootstrapped RL approaches
have been limited to robotic arms (6-7 DOF) with simple grippers. No prior
work has applied this methodology to high-DOF dexterous hands (20+ DOF) for
contact-rich manipulation.

2.2 Dexterous Manipulation with Deep RL
---------------------------------------
Rajeswaran et al. [1] demonstrated learning complex dexterous manipulation
with a 24-DOF Shadow Hand using human demonstrations and RL (DAPG algorithm).
OpenAI's Dactyl [2] achieved vision-based cube reorientation through pure RL
with domain randomization. DexPBT [6] scaled dexterous manipulation using
population-based training with Kuka+Allegro (7+16 DOF).

**Gap in Prior Work:** While these works successfully learned dexterous
manipulation, they relied on human demonstrations [1] or massive computational
resources (100 years of simulation [2]). None leveraged automated motion
planning for demonstration generation.

2.3 Reward Shaping for Contact-Rich Tasks
-----------------------------------------
Prior work on dexterous manipulation [1,2,6] identified challenges with
shortcut behaviors (e.g., pushing objects with arms instead of grasping).
Contact-aware reward mechanisms have been proposed [7,8] but not systematically
evaluated in the context of motion planning-bootstrapped RL.

3. MAIN IDEAS AND NOVELTY
================================================================================

3.1 Extension to High-DOF Dexterous Hands
-----------------------------------------
We extend motion planning-bootstrapped RL [3] to high-DOF dexterous hands:

- HDR20-DG5F: 26-DOF (6-DOF industrial arm + 20-DOF fully-actuated 5-finger)
- UH035-Inspire: 12-DOF (6-DOF industrial arm + 6-DOF under-actuated 5-finger)

**Why This Is Non-Trivial:**
1) Action space dimensionality increases 4× (7 DOF → 26 DOF)
2) Contact dynamics become critical (multi-finger grasping vs arm reaching)
3) Kinematic planning alone insufficient (must coordinate 5 fingers)

3.2 Automated Demonstration Generation Pipeline
----------------------------------------------
Following [3], we generate demonstrations automatically via MoveIt2:

Phase 1 (Isaac Sim + MoveIt2):
1. User specifies target object pose via GUI (mouse click)
2. MoveIt2 plans collision-free trajectory using RRT-Connect
3. Execute trajectory in Isaac Sim to validate kinematic feasibility
4. Export successful demonstrations: (state, action, reward, next_state)

Phase 2 (Isaac Lab):
5. Import demonstrations into Isaac Lab (4096 parallel environments)
6. Pre-train policy via behavior cloning (50 epochs)
7. RL fine-tuning with demonstration buffer + contact-aware rewards

3.3 Contact-Aware Reward Shaping (Novel Contribution)
----------------------------------------------------
We introduce three contact-specific reward terms to address shortcut behaviors:

1) Contact Gating: Position/orientation tracking rewards activate only when
   finger-object contact detected (prevents arm-pushing shortcuts)

   R_pos_gated = R_pos × I(contact_force > threshold)

2) Grasp Duration: Rewards sustained multi-finger contact (prevents momentary
   tapping)

   R_grasp_duration = w × I(contact_duration > min_steps)

3) Ground Contact Penalty: Penalizes object-table collisions (prevents
   bouncing exploits)

   R_ground_penalty = -w × I(object_table_contact)

**Key Insight:** Motion planning provides kinematically feasible initialization,
but contact-aware rewards guide RL to discover stable grasping strategies that
planners cannot capture.

3.4 Novelty vs Prior Work
-------------------------
Our approach differs from prior motion planning-bootstrapped RL in three ways:

1) **Robot Embodiment:** High-DOF dexterous hands (26/12 DOF) vs robotic arms
   (7 DOF) [3,4,5]

2) **Task Complexity:** Contact-rich multi-finger grasping vs free-space
   reaching or simple gripper tasks [3,4,5]

3) **Reward Design:** Contact-aware reward shaping tailored for dexterous
   manipulation vs standard position/orientation tracking [3,4,5]

4) **Framework:** Isaac Lab GPU-accelerated parallel simulation (4096 envs)
   vs PyBullet single-threaded [3] or real robot [5]

5) **Comparison Study:** Under-actuated vs fully-actuated hands (novel
   contribution not in any prior work)

6. RISK MITIGATION
================================================================================

Risk 1: MoveIt2 + Isaac Sim Integration Complexity
--------------------------------------------------
Mitigation: NVIDIA provides official tutorial [9]. If integration fails, use
Isaac Sim's built-in motion planner (RMPflow) as fallback.

Risk 2: Demonstration Quality for High-DOF Hands
------------------------------------------------
Mitigation: Motion planner may produce suboptimal finger trajectories. If BC
pre-training fails, skip Phase 2 BC and directly use demonstrations in replay
buffer during RL (DQfD-style [10]).

Risk 3: Insufficient Training Time (5 weeks)
--------------------------------------------
Mitigation: Prioritize core experiments (Pure PPO vs BC+PPO+Contact-Aware).
If time limited, reduce ablation study to 2 variants instead of 4.

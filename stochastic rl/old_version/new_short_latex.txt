\section{Motivation}

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated human-level performance in dexterous manipulation \cite{rajeswaran2018learning, andrychowicz2020learning}, but suffers from extreme sample inefficiency. For example, OpenAI Dactyl \cite{andrychowicz2020learning} required 100 years worth of simulation to learn cube rotation, severely hindering real-world deployment.

\subsection{Prior Solutions and Research Gap}

Recent works have proposed bootstrapping RL with automatic demonstrations from motion planning \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping, bhaskar2024planrl, lian2023reinforcement}. Abbatematteo et al.\ \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping} showed that kinematic planning-based demonstrations outperform random initialization, and PLANRL \cite{bhaskar2024planrl} achieved 90\% success on real robots. However, these approaches were limited to robotic arms with simple grippers (6--7 DOF).

Scaling to high-DOF dexterous hands (20+ DOF) presents three challenges: (1) 3--4$\times$ larger action space, (2) kinematic planners cannot capture contact dynamics, (3) multi-finger coordination requires stable grasping beyond kinematics alone. This research extends motion planning-bootstrapped RL to high-DOF hands with contact-aware reward shaping.

\section{Problem Description}

We use a UR10e arm (6-DOF) with two dexterous hands: \textbf{UR10e-DG5F} (26-DOF, fully-actuated) and \textbf{UR10e-Inspire} (12-DOF, under-actuated). We learn object lifting tasks using RL-Games PPO with GPU acceleration (4096 parallel environments) in Isaac Lab.

\subsection{Three-Phase Framework}

\textbf{Phase 1: Automated Demonstration Generation.} We use MoveIt2 to plan collision-free trajectories, execute them in Isaac Sim, and record (state, action) pairs. We generate 1000 demonstrations per robot.

\textbf{Phase 2: Behavior Cloning Pre-training.} We pre-train a 3-layer FC network (256-256-128) on demonstrations for 50 epochs with MSE loss, targeting $>$30\% success rate.

\textbf{Phase 3: RL Fine-tuning + Contact Rewards.} We perform PPO training (5000 epochs) with contact-specific rewards, maintaining a demonstration buffer (25\% demo + 75\% RL data).

\subsection{Core Contribution: Contact-Aware Reward Shaping}

To address shortcut behaviors (arm pushing, floor bouncing), we introduce three contact-specific rewards: (1) \textbf{Contact Gating} activates position tracking only during finger-object contact, (2) \textbf{Grasp Duration} encourages sustained multi-finger contact (minimum 10 steps), (3) \textbf{Ground Penalty} penalizes object-floor collisions. \textbf{Key Insight:} Motion planning provides kinematic feasibility while contact rewards provide dynamic stability, enabling robust grasping.

\subsection{Experimental Design}

We conduct an ablation study with 4 variants: (1) Pure PPO (baseline), (2) BC + PPO, (3) PPO + Contact, (4) BC + PPO + Contact (full method). We evaluate success rate, sample efficiency (samples to 80\% success), contact forces, and grasp quality across 2 robots (8 total experiments). We expect 2--4$\times$ faster convergence and 80\%+ success versus pure PPO.

\section{Expected Contribution}

\subsection{Theoretical/Algorithmic Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Scaling to High-DOF Systems:} Extends motion planning-bootstrapped RL \cite{abbatematteo2021bootstrapping} from 7-DOF arms to 26-DOF dexterous hands, validating sample efficiency gains in high dimensions.

    \item \textbf{Kinematic-Dynamic Complementarity:} Demonstrates that combining kinematic planning (collision avoidance) with contact rewards (stable grasping) addresses individual limitations.

    \item \textbf{Automated Demonstration Pipeline:} Presents fully automated demonstration generation without human teleoperation.

    \item \textbf{Contact-Aware Reward Framework:} Provides systematic reward shaping for dexterous manipulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Practical Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Sample Efficiency:} Pure PPO requires $\sim$100M samples (24 hours) for 80\% success; our method achieves this with 25--50M samples (6--12 hours), providing 2--4$\times$ speedup and 50--75\% time reduction.

    \item \textbf{Cost Reduction:} Automated generation (\$0 hardware, 5--9 hours total) versus human demonstrations (\$10,000+ hardware, 8--83 hours) or pure RL (100 years simulation, \$3,500+ electricity).

    \item \textbf{Sim-to-Real Validation:} Validates transfer from UR10e simulation to real robots, demonstrating rapid adaptation to novel objects.

    \item \textbf{Open-Source Toolkit:} Provides demonstration toolkit, reward library, and implementation guide for Isaac Lab community.
\end{enumerate}

This research advances sample efficiency in high-DOF robot learning, enhances understanding of motion planning and RL integration, provides reward shaping guidelines for contact manipulation, and improves accessibility of RL technology for practical robotics applications.
